# Event Setup — hardware & booth

## The screen: Apolosign 32" Smart Portable TV
- Android 16 (Google Play, Chrome), octa-core processor, 16GB RAM
- 32" 10-point touchscreen, 1080p (or 4K model), 300 nits
- Rotates landscape ↔ portrait, tilts ±20°, height adjust ~18cm, on a 5-wheel rolling stand
- Built-in battery, up to ~6 hrs (tested by maker at 50% brightness — expect less at full brightness with the camera and 3D running)
- HDMI input (for laptop), Wi-Fi, Bluetooth
- 8MP camera with mic, USB-A cable, mounts on the top edge (portrait). Also works plugged into a laptop.
- Product page: https://www.apolosign.com/products/apolosign-32-inch-portable-smart-screen

## Two ways to run it

### Mode A — TV only (goal, if the benchmark passes)
Nothing but the TV on its stand. App runs in Chrome on the TV.
1. Turn the TV to **portrait**. Attach camera on top, plug its USB into the TV.
2. Open the **Grid City Mirror** app icon on the home screen (installed once from Chrome).
3. It opens fullscreen and starts in the lineup screen. Done.
- Pros: no cords, no laptop, rolls anywhere.
- Cons: performance depends on the TV's chip — Phase 1 benchmark decides.
- Charge fully the night before. Bring the power brick for long events.

### Mode B — Laptop + TV (guaranteed fallback)
1. TV in portrait. Camera on top, but plug the camera's USB into the **laptop** (needs a USB-C → USB-A adapter for the MacBook Pro).
2. HDMI cable from laptop to TV (MacBook: USB-C → HDMI adapter). On the TV, switch input to HDMI.
3. On the Mac: System Settings → Displays → set the TV to "Use as main display" or mirror, and rotate it 90° to match portrait.
4. Double-click **Start Grid City Mirror** on the desktop. It opens fullscreen on the TV.
- Keep the laptop plugged in and set it to never sleep during the event.

## Booth layout tips (affects tracking quality)
- **Distance:** guests should stand ~2–3 m (6–10 ft) back for full body. Put a floor sticker / mat with "STAND HERE".
- **Camera height:** top of a 32" portrait screen is roughly at head height — good. Tilt the screen slightly down if feet are cut off.
- **Background:** plain wall or backdrop behind guests helps. Avoid a busy aisle walking behind them (it can grab the wrong person).
- **Light:** light on the guests' front, not behind them. Avoid a bright window behind the guest.
- **Crowd control:** a rope or tape line so only 1–2 people are in the "play zone" at a time.
